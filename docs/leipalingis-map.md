# Leipalingis map

## Current map: the real Leipalingis area, adapted for play

Generated by `scripts/generate-leipalingis-map.py` from the OpenStreetMap snapshot (`src/game/maps/leipalingis-osm.json`) into `src/game/maps/leipalingis-map.json`. Run `python3 scripts/generate-leipalingis-map.py`, then `npm test`.

- **Real geography**: real roads, lakes, woods, fields, villages and farmsteads (farmsteads thinned to about 40%).
- **Roads repaired**: road ends within 4 cells of another road are joined; isolated fragments removed. Main roads never dead-end inside the playable area (tested); remaining dead ends are farm/forest tracks and cul-de-sacs (under 15% of road ends, tested).
- **Two large towns**: Leipalingis (real) and Seirijai, added on route 180 by its lake (Seirijai lies north of Leipalingis on route 180; the distance is compressed to fit).
- **River** west-east between the towns; bridges where roads cross, muddy fords where farm tracks cross.
- **Hills** from a coarse height map (valleys along the river and lakes). Ridges block line of sight, high ground sees 20% further and hits harder (tested).
- **Woods**: wood-edge cells give the best cover and concealment; deep woods hide well. Villages sit in clearings (no forest in a village core, tested).
- **Buildings**: houses, brick houses, apartment blocks (large towns), barns, workshops, churches. Every village and town house stands on a street (within 2 cells) and is turned to face the road segment nearest to it, so rows follow curved streets; no house within one cell of water; lone farmsteads keep a free cell round them (all tested).
- **Town layout**: outside the Leipalingis and Seirijai cores no two houses share a side, so each keeps a garden; street houses stand right on the street (paved or gravel lane), with barns and town back lots at most two cells back and clear of other buildings. Every settlement keeps an open square at its centre, so its objective is never walled into a pocket. Only the two town cores have joined rows and built-up back lots.
- **Water**: each lake has one flat surface just below its shore; the river follows its banks (tested; lakes used to sit 2-3 units down in pits).
- **Roads** are drawn at house-scale widths (main road 0.7 cells, village street 0.42).
- **Not mirrored. Fair by measurement**: mains west (34, 119) and east (392, 149) were chosen by a travel-time search (equal time to Leipalingis, Seirijai within ~5%, tanks within ~4%); the 16 RAAS lanes in `src/game/maps/raasLeipalingis.ts` were chosen the same way and are re-checked in `src/test/leipalingis-raas.test.ts`.

## Geographic source and scale

- Source: [OpenStreetMap API](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/API_v0.6), downloaded 2026-09-21.
- Bounds: west 23.710°, south 54.025°, east 24.020°, north 54.160°; approximately 20 × 15 geographic kilometres, projected north-up onto the 440 × 320 gameplay grid.
- Imported features: primary/secondary/local road and longer track centre lines, complete forest/field/water polygons including multipolygon holes, building centres and settlement coordinates. Roads 180, 133, 134 and 2505 retain their real topology.
- This is a **compressed tactical adaptation**, not a survey-scale digital twin. Road widths are exaggerated for vehicle clearance. Buildings are representative models, thinned and moved by up to two cells when necessary. Staging clearings, fighting positions, field vegetation and cover props are gameplay additions. Some small features and incomplete multipolygons are omitted. Heights are procedural, not surveyed elevation data.
- Jovaišiai is north of Leipalingis; Didžiasalis is south; Veršiai is south-east; Stračiūnai is north-east. Seirijai is outside the crop, so the fifth deployment is labelled **Seirijų kryptis**, on the northern route 180 approach.
- Deployment and gameplay use the same mission seed. Displayed distances are approximate geographic separation, not the length of a computed gameplay route.

Map data © [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), licensed under [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). The derived database is `src/game/maps/leipalingis-osm.json`; that licence applies to the geographic data. The interface also displays attribution.

To refresh the source with Python 3, using a persistent download cache:

```sh
python scripts/import-leipalingis.py /tmp/fbc-osm-cache
```

The importer uses 12 bounded API requests, at most two concurrently. Raw responses stay in the specified cache. It strips contributor account metadata and emits only map features. Review regenerated data and run tests before replacing the bundled snapshot.

## Movement, exposure and cover

| Surface/object | Gameplay effect |
| --- | --- |
| Asphalt | Base movement ×1.46 (`traction / movementCost`), fatigue accumulation ×0.70, detection accumulation ×1.50, no cover |
| Gravel track | Base movement ×0.89; narrow woodland tracks may only admit infantry |
| Grass shoulder | Ordinary off-road movement; wide vehicular road corridors remain clear of blocking props |
| Mixed woodland | Slower infantry movement, concealment, impassable to ground vehicles |
| Timber stack / stone wall / truck / barricade | Occupy and hold; reduces incoming accuracy and improves infantry cover |
| Hay bales | More concealment, less ballistic protection than stone walls |
| Farmhouse / courtyard | Occupy and hold; can fire from the occupied cell, but buildings between shooter and target block LOS |
| Trench | Existing strong infantry cover; vehicles route around it |

Speed figures describe terrain alone; fatigue, suppression, turning and formation cohesion still affect actual speed. Pathfinding minimises travel time. Its heuristic and smoothing preserve worthwhile road detours rather than replacing them with straight field crossings. Paths still respect diagonal obstacles and vehicle footprints.

Select infantry and **right-click a cover object** to assign separate reachable positions near it. Infantry hold their positions on arrival. If there are fewer available positions than selected troops, the remaining units receive an ordinary nearby move. Shift+right-click retains waypoint queuing; Ctrl+right-click retains attack-move. A new order cancels the hold-on-arrival intent. Props currently provide cover, not supplies or destructibility.

## Settlements and scale

The first expansion doubles both gameplay dimensions from 220 × 160 to **440 × 320**: 140,800 cells and four times the playable area, with the same geographic bounds. Soldiers, vehicles, houses, road widths and local combat ranges retain their scale. Travel distances grow; movement speeds are not globally doubled. The source JSON remains in its original projection (`grid: [220,160]`); `geography.ts` reprojects roads, polygons/holes, building centres and places together at runtime.

With mission seed 1917, the expanded generator places 217 buildings in Leipalingis, 86 in Jovaišiai, 80 in Didžiasalis, 33 in Veršiai and 83 in Stračiūnai, plus 168 rural buildings. There are 178 adjacent gardens, orchards and service yards. Buildings are representative, not one-for-one real footprints. Added yards remain traversable and do not replace road cells; nearby walls and farm props retain the occupy/hold interactions.

The landscape is divided into 140 sections of up to 32 × 32 cells. Only sections intersecting the camera view plus a margin allocate terrain, vegetation, buildings, water and road textures. All units continue simulating globally. Road textures have matching gutters and global dash coordinates at section borders, with a maximum tile size of 576 × 576 instead of one large map texture. Heights and tint noise use global coordinates. Sections release resources when removed from view. A* reuses indexed search storage and limits shortcut lookahead for longer orders; it remains grid routing, not a new hierarchical navigation system.


The shared settlement definitions preserve five built-up centres: Leipalingis, Jovaišiai, Didžiasalis, Veršiai and Stračiūnai. Representative buildings cluster around imported streets, with sparser farms between villages. Roof/wall palettes and church, civic, farm or workshop landmarks distinguish them. These are stylised identities, not surveyed architectural claims. Settlement ellipses describe tactical extents, not administrative borders.

Deployments now start on nearby approach roads outside the centres. Building generation no longer clears out the villages for spawning. The deployment map, in-game clickable regional map and projected world names share settlement coordinates. Click the regional map to focus the camera; Home returns to the force.

Soldiers, vehicle models, selection rings and cover props have been rescaled relative to houses. Houses have lower rural proportions. Vehicle routing admits single-cell streets while continuous hull checks and route smoothing prevent wall clipping. This is a compressed tactical scale; movement/exposure still use terrain cells and are approximate at road edges.

See [game direction](game-direction.md) for the agreed Broken Arrow presentation and future Squad-inspired pace/modes. This change does not implement Invasion or multiplayer.

## Visual changes

- A single terrain-composited road texture joins asphalt and shoulders without stacked meshes or depth fighting. Centre dashes stop around junctions. Narrower imported roads share their geometry with both tactical maps.
- Mixed pine, broadleaf and birch trees, variable crowns, pale birch trunks, undergrowth and meadow blades replace uniform cone forests.
- Batched farmhouses have pitched roofs, chimneys, windows, steps and fences; five kinds of cover have distinct models and open firing slots.
- Procedural grain adds local ground/asphalt detail without external texture downloads.
- High/Standard graphics controls pixel density and shadow resolution. Shadows concentrate on the camera view for sharper local detail.
- Initial camera focuses the deployed force. **Home** focuses selected troops, or the remaining army if nothing is selected.

## Run and check this branch

```sh
git fetch origin
git switch feat/expanded-leipalingis
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. Proceed to deployment, choose a starting location and deploy.

1. Compare the deployment road network with [OpenStreetMap around Leipalingis](https://www.openstreetmap.org/#map=12/54.0920/23.8598).
2. Pause with Space. Box-select infantry, right-click a timber stack or stone wall, then resume. The log should report taking cover; units should occupy different nearby positions and hold.
3. Select a unit on asphalt. Its HUD/readout shows the speed/exposure tradeoff. Compare with an off-road move; a worthwhile road detour should remain in the route.
4. Pan/zoom through a forest edge and a village. Check road bends, roofs, tree variety, cover props and shadows. Toggle High/Standard and use Home to return to the force.
5. Try a tank/APC route from each deployment to the objective. Forest and water crossings must follow passable lanes.

Automated checks: `npm test`, `npx tsc -p tsconfig.app.json --noEmit`, `npm run build`. The map regression suite covers road travel time, route smoothing, detection exposure, blocked destinations, cover occupancy/hold, ballistic cover, building LOS, geography, expanded town density, yard access, section coverage, and actual infantry/tank/APC travel from all five staging areas without vehicle hull clipping. The expansion browser check covers deployment, initial rendering, pause, and camera travel from staging to Leipalingis and back to Jovaišiai. At the default close view it loaded 12–13 of the 140 sections, with no JavaScript or shader errors. This is a visibility/resource check, not a hardware FPS benchmark. Full hardware FPS benchmarking and a complete mission playthrough remain manual checks.
